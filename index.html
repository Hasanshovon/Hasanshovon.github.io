Efficient inference · Vision-language · Robotic manipulation

Making large models small enough to run where the work happens.

I work on the cost side of modern deep learning: quantization and inference-time strategies that let transformer models keep their accuracy at a fraction of the compute — and on the perception systems that need exactly that, running in a control loop on real hardware.

My background runs from EEG signal classification through computer vision to language models, which is why efficiency is the thread I keep pulling: on a robot, latency is not a benchmark column, it is whether the grasp succeeds.

Visuo-tactile slip detection and closed-loop corrective grasping

FAST-Lab, Tampere University · manuscript in preparation

A four-class slip classifier (stable, translational, clockwise, anticlockwise) running live on an ABB manipulator. Visual and tactile streams are encoded by separate video transformer backbones and fused with multi-head attention; a state machine turns each prediction into a graded grip correction and verifies recovery before releasing the object. The interesting part is not the classifier — it is what happens when you close the loop and the model has to be right within a few milliseconds, repeatedly, on hardware that does not wait.

ViViT + TimeSformer MHA fusion 4-class ABB + RealSense D405 13–15 ms/window

Quantization of small open language models

Benchmarking study · targeting IEEE conference submission

A controlled comparison of post-training quantization methods — GPTQ, AWQ, and sparse-quantized representations — across the Qwen and Llama small-model families. Published numbers are rarely comparable across papers: different calibration sets, different task suites, different hardware. The work builds a single evaluation harness so the accuracy–latency–memory trade-off can actually be read off, and looks at where outlier isolation stops paying for itself.

GPTQ · AWQ · SpQR Qwen / Llama accuracy–latency–memory

Inference-time strategies for small vision transformers

Ongoing · survey and empirical study

Token pruning, token merging, early exit, and resolution scaling applied to DeiT-Small and ViT-Small backbones. The question is how far a small pretrained model can be pushed at inference time before retraining a larger one becomes the cheaper option — and whether these strategies compose, or quietly cancel each other out.

DeiT-S / ViT-S token pruning & merging early exit

Hallucination detection by neuro-symbolic verification

Ongoing · evaluated on HaluEval

Neural detectors flag hallucinated responses well on average and badly on the minority class that matters. This work pairs a learned detector with an explicit verification stage — claim extraction, symbolic checking against structured knowledge — and asks whether the symbolic half earns its complexity, or whether a single-pass distilled detector recovers most of it.
